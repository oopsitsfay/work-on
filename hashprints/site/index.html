01 About

No reveal.
No pre-made art.

The art doesn't exist until someone mines it. Every print is rendered from the keccak256 hash its minter found, and the drawing code runs right here in your browser, so anyone can redraw any token from its on-chain seed.

Minting takes two inputs: a nonce for which keccak256(contract, wallet, nonce) starts with enough zero bits, and the mint price.

The contract checks the hash, stores it as the token's seed, and mints. Your wallet address is part of the hash, so nobody can copy your nonce, and the same seed always gives the same image.

The result is a collection where every piece carries a small proof of the work that made it.

02 Minting

Run. Found.
Mint.

Every mint costs the same. The work you put in decides whether you can mint and how rare the result is. It never changes the price.

01 Run

Start a run

Your browser mines nonces until keccak256(contract, wallet, nonce) starts with the run's required zero bits. Free, nothing on-chain. At the start of the mint that takes about a second on a laptop.

02 Found

Find a hash

When a hash meets the target, the miner stops and shows the print it draws. Keep it, or run again for more zero bits and a rarer finish.

03 Mint

Mint it

Send the nonce with exactly 0.000315 ETH plus gas. The contract checks the hash, saves it as the seed and mints the print to you. Up to 2 per wallet.

Runs and difficulty

The 3,330 public prints are minted in 10 runs of 333. Difficulty starts at 16 zero bits and goes up by one every two runs, ending at 20. Each extra bit doubles the average work.

Times assume about 150,000 hashes per second, roughly what a laptop browser manages.

Rarity

If your hash has more zero bits than the run requires, the print is rarer. Stop at the first valid hash, or keep searching for a Gold Leaf or Holo Foil finish. The odds are for stopping at the first valid hash.

03 The art

Drawn from
the hash.

Each print is a 32 × 32 image drawn by a seeded walk over the grid. The look borrows from risograph printing: two inks, slight misregistration, and a third colour where the inks overlap.

1 2 3 4 5
  1. Work marksOne mark per leading zero bit in the hash. Dim marks are the required bits, bright marks are extra.
  2. Drawing areaA 28 × 26 px field where the lines are drawn.
  3. Overlap and driftThe second ink can sit a pixel off. Where the inks overlap they mix into a third colour.
  4. Hash strip28 bits of the hash, printed as a barcode.
  5. StockPaper or Night background with a light grain and a 2 px margin.

06 FAQ

Questions.

Anything else, ask on X. We'll never DM you first.

How much does it cost?

0.000315 ETH per mint, about $0.80 when the price was set (ETH ≈ $2,540, October 12, 2026). Gas is separate and depends on the network when you mint. A mint uses about 124,000 gas.

Does finding more zero bits make it cheaper?

No. Everyone pays the same price. Extra zero bits only affect rarity.

Why 2 per wallet?

To spread the collection across more holders. It's per address, so it can't stop someone using several wallets, but each wallet needs its own hash search.

Can a GPU find hashes faster?

Yes. Faster hardware finds valid and rare hashes sooner. A laptop browser finds a 16-bit hash in under a second, and a Gold Leaf one in a few seconds.

Can I pick what my print looks like?

Not directly. Each nonce gives a different hash and a different image. You can keep searching until a valid one you like turns up.

What's stored on-chain?

Each token's 32-byte seed. The difficulty is worked out from the token id. Images and traits are rendered from the seed by hashprints.js.

How do marketplaces get the image?

tokenURI points to /api/meta/<id>?seed=…&d=… on this site, with the seed taken from the contract. The API renders the traits and an SVG from the seed. It doesn't read the chain or use a database.

Can I trade here?

Yes. Owners can list prints, accept offers and make collection offers on the collection page. Trades settle on Seaport 1.6 with an 8% creator fee, and the site never holds your funds.

What are artist proofs?

Tokens #1–3, reserved for the artist. They're free, but they still needed a valid 16-bit hash. Public mints start at #4.
